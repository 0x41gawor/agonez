Create a single standardized visual-signature image for the Agonez exercise atlas.

The image must depict the named resistance-training exercise clearly and recognizably.

## Visual style

Use a consistent technical fitness illustration style:

- realistic but slightly stylized 3D human figure
- adult male athlete
- natural, proportionally muscular physique
- advanced recreational lifter, not bodybuilding-stage muscularity
- anatomically plausible proportions
- neutral appearance and expression
- clean, premium, scientific visual language
- light neutral studio background
- subtle soft shadows only
- restrained desaturated palette
- human body primarily in warm-neutral / light-gray tones
- training equipment in dark graphite / charcoal tones
- no dramatic cinematic lighting
- no gym environment or background clutter
- no mirrors
- no other people
- no branding
- no logos
- no text
- no labels
- no arrows
- no muscle heatmaps
- no annotations

The result should resemble a high-quality visual asset from a modern biomechanics or exercise-analysis application, not fitness advertising.

## Composition

Show the athlete and all exercise-critical equipment clearly.

The exercise must be immediately recognizable from the image alone.

Use:

- a clean 3/4 camera angle when appropriate
- otherwise choose the angle that best communicates the exercise
- consistent medium-distance framing
- entire relevant body position visible
- all resistance-generating equipment visible
- no unnecessary empty space
- subject and equipment centered as one visual composition
- approximately 75–85% of the usable frame occupied by the exercise scene

Use the same general camera, lighting, body proportions, color palette and rendering language that would make this image look like part of a large coherent library of hundreds of exercise illustrations.

## Exercise correctness

Prioritize biomechanical and equipment correctness over visual drama.

Infer the conventional meaning of the provided exercise name and resistance source.

Accurately depict where applicable:

- body orientation
- seated / standing / lying / kneeling position
- bench inclination
- grip orientation and width
- arm position
- elbow position
- leg position
- foot placement
- torso orientation
- relevant joint configuration
- cable origin and cable direction
- pulley position
- machine geometry
- barbell or dumbbell placement
- resistance direction
- relevant support surfaces

Do not invent unusual exercise variations unless explicitly implied by the exercise name.

If the exercise has a commonly recognized standardized form, depict that form.

For unilateral exercises, clearly depict the exercise as unilateral.

For cable exercises, ensure the cable visibly connects the handle or attachment to a physically plausible pulley.

For machine exercises, show a plausible machine corresponding to the resistance source and exercise.

For bodyweight exercises, include only equipment actually required to perform the movement.

## Movement phase

Depict one representative, highly recognizable working position of the exercise.

Prefer the position that best communicates what exercise is being performed rather than attempting to illustrate motion.

Do not show multiple ghosted positions or motion sequences.

## Consistency requirements

This image is one member of a large exercise-atlas collection.

Therefore consistency across different exercises is critical.

Maintain:

- the same athlete archetype
- the same approximate physique
- the same rendering style
- the same background
- the same equipment color language
- the same lighting
- the same level of realism
- the same visual density

Avoid introducing stylistic variation based on the exercise itself.

Create exactly one clean exercise illustration suitable for use as:

- an exercise card visual signature
- an exercise detail-page hero image
